# Storage Rush — Story Walkthrough

For Mike (Storage Auction Pirate). Walkthrough only. Nothing has been built.

You open the app and you're already in the yard. A row of orange roll-up doors sits in the sun. One big button: Open Unit.

You walk up to Unit 12. A cheap lock hangs on the latch. You tap it. The lock snaps. The orange door rolls up with that familiar rattle, and light pours into a packed room. This is not a flat cartoon board. You're looking into a real-looking 3D storage unit: blue totes, cardboard boxes, wooden crates, a bike wheel sticking out of the pile. It feels like the second after a winning bid.

The first level is easy on purpose. You swap two neighboring items to make a line of three or more of the same thing. Matched totes, boxes, or crates lift off the pile and land on your cart. A gold glint shows in the back. That's what makes this its own game: you're digging a unit, not clearing pretty shapes for a score. Every good match opens space, and buried finds start to show.

A level is a short cleanout with a limited number of moves. The goal is plain: pull the marked finds and clear a path to the back wall. When a valuable find is uncovered — a guitar, a jewelry box, a sealed safe — you tap it onto the cart before the pile settles back over it. Leave a prize sitting too long, and a Scrapper can snatch it.

Between levels you roll the cart to the flip table. Sold items turn into cash in your bank. The door behind you rolls down, and you walk to the next unit with a new bid card in hand. On the fence, a painted sign says, "You can't hit a home run unless you swing the bat."

The rooms change as you go. Early units are clean and climate-controlled. Then a greasy garage with daylight from the driveway. Later, outdoor lockers in a hot auction yard, a warehouse aisle, a barn, a loaded moving truck. Same simple matching, but new rooms, new light, and junk that looks like stuff you'd actually buy at a sale. Fresh levels can keep coming in updates, so the rush doesn't run out.

Your bank grows. Better units take a bigger bid but hide better finds. You collect rare pieces and unlock new yards. The game stays simple: swap, fill the cart, flip for cash, open the next door.

After a few free units you come to a closed orange door with the lock still on. A sign beside it says, "Scared money don't make money." That's the pay moment. You pay, and the door rolls up, like cutting the lock on a unit you just won. How it makes money is your call. It could be a $4.99 one-time unlock, same as Pirate Arcade, with no subscriptions and no trials. Or it could start free, with that same unlock after the first units, and optional hint packs later. Nothing is set until you say so.

## Open decisions

1. Should Storage Rush be $4.99 up front, or free at first with a one-time unlock at the locked door?
2. How many free units should someone play before that door?
3. Do you want Scrappers stealing finds, or keep the game peaceful and just about the units?
4. Should the flip table be its own short scene between units, or just a cash number on screen?
5. Do the yards use made-up facility names, or pirate-branded lots only?
